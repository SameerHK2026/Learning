The rains have made everything lush green.

As I drive towards the ghats, the intensity of the rain increases. So does the greenery.

I stop at a viewpoint where several people have halted to take pictures.
The scene is beautiful.

Green fields. Dark clouds. Mist.

I pause before a lake created by backwaters of a dam.

Rains and wind make light waves on the water surface.

I take my mobile out and take pictures. I must have clicked 15–20 pictures.

And then I paused …

Why am I clicking? Memories? Do I really need to photograph everything I see?

Will the memory be the same because I have a photograph of it?

Or is it better to soak it all in and create a memory through experience?

After all, the screen is two-dimensional.

What I was seeing was three-dimensional.

Richer in detail. Richer in colour. Richer in experience.

It reminded me of the movie The Secret Life of Walter Mitty.

The movie has great narrative and messaging.

The protagonist is chasing an elusive photographer because of a missing photograph.

If you love photography, watch it for that alone. Anyone who loves the camera should not miss it for the sheer passion that a character has for photography.

At the end, the protagonist catches up with him on a Himalayan mountain, tracking the evasive snow leopard.

The leopard appears.

The photographer has the leopard in the frame and shows it to the protagonist.
And then,

He lowers the camera.

Does not click!

Just marvels at the beauty of the majestic animal, the nature around, the moment!

Is it possible for me to forget the camera for a moment…

…and use the one given to me by nature?

My own eyes.
