Is the "age of the virtual" over?

A few years back, supposedly the airline industry faced an unforeseen challenge.

Not the rising fuel cost.

Not the low-cost airlines.

Virtual meetings!

You could get on a video call and have an almost in-person conversation.

People wouldn't need to travel at all!

(Though this has not been the case. People still ended up travelling.)

Then came COVID. Travel scares. Remote work became the norm. Video call technology became ubiquitous.

For some time, I was afraid people would forget what a "pat on the back" actually means.

There is a subtle shift over the last couple of years.

There are virtual events, with their enormous advantage of joining from anywhere and saving travel time.

But this has not replaced in-person events in totality.

People have started going to the office to connect with other people.

Chat in person.

Eat at the same table.

We missed this social connection.

And then came 2026.

A phenomenal year with every second word in a sentence being "AI."

The features were already there, but this year they have really taken off.

Voice, picture, video — anything can be generated, copied, mimicked.

Very soon, there would be bots, our virtual clones, who can look like us, talk like us, even behave like us!

This brings up an interesting question.

How do we convince someone that we are who we claim we are?

Will this lead to more people preferring physical presence, at least where identity is critical?

Will a handshake become more valuable?

Will being physically present become a way of establishing trust?

Perhaps the irony is that technology first made physical presence less necessary.
And now, technology may make it more valuable.

Is the "age of the virtual" slowly getting over?
